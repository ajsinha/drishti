# Knowledge

*See what your data hasn't told you yet.* These pages say what the words of a bank's data mean, and how Drishti's data connects
them, so you can read a number and know where it comes from and what else it touches. Each page covers a few related subjects, written for a reader new
to them, with real numbers from the demo data and the commands that show them.

## The pages

| Page | What it explains |
|---|---|
| [Risk](risk.md) | [market risk](risk.md#1-market-risk): VaR and expected shortfall, P&L vectors and historical simulation, positions, portfolios, hierarchies, contributions, backtesting, stress and FRTB in brief; [counterparty credit risk](risk.md#2-counterparty-credit-risk): counterparties, agreements, CSAs, netting sets, collateral, exposure (EE, PFE, EPE, EEPE), limits, CVA, SA-CCR and SIMM in brief; [trades and products](risk.md#3-trades-and-products): a trade and its booking systems, statuses, products, legs and events, books, desks and traders, valuations |
| [Data](data.md) | [market data](data.md#1-market-data): curves, fixings, FX, volatility surfaces, credit curves, equities and indices, correlations and beta; [entities and ownership](data.md#2-entities-and-ownership): the LEI, GLEIF parents and ownership groups, the bank's legal entities, counterparties and their groups, issuers; [data and contracts](data.md#3-data-and-contracts): packs, kinds and commands, fundamental against computed data, data contracts, business dates, where data comes from |
| [World](world.md) | [companies, funds and ETFs](world.md#1-companies-funds-and-etfs): tickers, CIK, SIC codes and divisions, share classes, EDGAR filings; mutual funds, ETFs, NAV, look-through holdings, expense ratios, index weights, active share, fund overlap; [economies](world.md#2-economies): GDP, growth, inflation, debt, forecasts, country groups, and how counterparties link to their economies |

## How a page is laid out

Each page has one numbered section per subject, and each subject the same five parts:

1. **In plain words**: what it is and why a bank cares.
2. **How the data connects**: the kinds, their links and their id conventions, from the packs' data contracts.
3. **A worked example on the demo data**: real numbers you can reproduce, with their business date.
4. **Questions to ask your data**: ready-made commands and what each reveals.
5. **Common confusions**: the mix-ups that cost people time.

After the subjects come the page's **Glossary** (each term, one line, and the command that shows it) and **Where to go deeper** (the
guides, tutorials and contracts).

## WHAT: ask from the command line

Type `WHAT` and press Enter to open this page. Type `WHAT` and a term to open its glossary entry: `WHAT PFE`, `WHAT netting set`,
`WHAT expected shortfall`. Case does not matter, and the term's long name and usual synonyms work too (`WHAT value at risk` opens
VaR). Words that are not a term open a search of these pages (`WHAT collateral posted`). On a data page, the **What does this mean?**
link beside the help **?** opens the page that explains it: the VaR explorer, `VRES` and `DESK` open Risk's market-risk part; `CPTY`, `NSET`,
`CSA` and the exposure explorer open its counterparty-credit-risk part.

## For writers

The glossary is one data file, [glossary.yaml](glossary.yaml): a term, its long name, synonyms, its page, a one-line meaning and the
command that shows it. `python3 tools/knowledge.py` writes every page's Glossary section and the A to Z above from it;
`python3 tools/knowledge.py --check` fails when a page differs, and the console's `WHAT` reads the same file, so the two never drift.
Add a term there, never in a page. Every command a page names must work on the demo lake that `drishti.py lake demo` builds.

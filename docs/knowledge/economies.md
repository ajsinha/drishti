## 1. The idea in plain words

Every counterparty lives in an economy. When that economy shrinks, prices race ahead, or the government cannot pay its debts, firms
there find it harder to pay theirs, and their currency may fall. A credit officer reading a counterparty therefore wants its
**country of risk** and the economy behind it: how big it is (**GDP**), how fast it grows (**real growth**), how fast prices rise
(**inflation**), how many people lack work (**unemployment**), how indebted the state is (**debt to GDP**), and whether the country lives
beyond its means abroad (**current account**). Where it is heading matters as much: the IMF's **World Economic Outlook** projects these
figures five years ahead.

Drishti's world-economy pack brings this public data together: World Bank indicators, IMF forecasts and the CIA World Factbook's
government data, one record per economy, with **country groups** (World Bank regions, income groups and the world) computed from the
members when opened. Each counterparty links to its economy, so the step from *who owes us* to *what is happening where they live* is
one click.

## 3. How the data connects

```text
counterparty  CPTY CP-<name>   ECONOMY ─────────────┐        (counterparty-risk and core packs)
legal-entity  LE LE-<city>     JURISDICTION ─────┐  │
                                                 │  ▼
economy            ECON ECON-<ISO3>          GDP, growth, inflation, unemployment, debt, current account, sectors
   │  REGION_GROUP, INCOME_GROUP ─► country-group  ECG ECG-<code>     computed: a region, an income group, or ECG-WLD the world
   │  OUTLOOK ─► economic-forecast  FCST FCST-<ISO3>-WEO               IMF projections to 2031 (internal use only)
   │  INDICATORS ─► macro-indicator  MACRO MACRO-<ISO3>-<series>       one statistic's series, latest value and rank
   │  LABOUR ─► labour-market  LABR LABR-<ISO3>      FISCAL ─► fiscal-position  FISC FISC-<ISO3>
   ◄── region-economy  REGE REGE-<country>-<region>   (a state or province)
                                                 ▼
jurisdiction       JUR JUR-<ISO3>            government, capital, population, legislature
   │  REGION ─► region  REGN REGN-<code>     CHIEF_OF_STATE, HEAD_OF_GOVERNMENT ─► office-holder OFFC
   │  ELECTIONS, LAST_ELECTION ─► election ELEC     PARTIES ─► party PARTY     SOCIAL_INDICATORS ─► social-indicator SOCI
```

The links are the contract link columns of `config/packs/world-economy/contracts/` and `config/packs/core/contracts/counterparty.contract.yaml`
(`ECONOMY → economy`). Companies link the same way: a listed company's and a US company's `ECONOMY` is its home economy.

**Id conventions.** Economies use the ISO 3166 three-letter code: `ECON-GBR`, `FCST-GBR-WEO`, `FISC-GBR`, `JUR-GBR`. Country groups
use the World Bank's codes: regions such as `ECG-ECS` (Europe and Central Asia), income groups such as `ECG-HIC` (high income), and
`ECG-WLD` for the world.

## 4. A worked example on the demo data

The world-economy pack's public data (World Bank WDI updated 2026-10-08, IMF World Economic Outlook April 2026, CIA World Factbook;
retrieved 2026-10-10). These records are not dated by business date: every date reads the same snapshot.

**From a counterparty to its economy.** `CPTY CP-ALDERSHOT <GO>`: Aldershot Pension Trust, a UK pension fund rated AA-, country of risk
GB. Its `ECONOMY` link is `ECON-GBR`:

| `ECON ECON-GBR` | Value | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| GDP (2025) | 4,002.6 bn USD | the 5th largest of 213 economies; 3,695.5 bn in 2024, 3,420.8 bn in 2023 |
| GDP per capita | 57,602 USD | 69.5 million people |
| Real growth | 1.39% | after 0.27% in 2023 and 1.08% in 2024 |
| Inflation | 3.88% | consumer prices, above the Bank of England's 2% target |
| Unemployment | 4.75% | ILO estimate |
| Government debt | 102.3% of GDP | 2025, the same figure as `FISC FISC-GBR` |
| Current account | −3.13% of GDP | the UK borrows from abroad to pay for it |
| Region and income group | `ECG-ECS` (Europe and Central Asia), `ECG-HIC` (high income) | its two country groups |

**Its outlook.** `FCST FCST-GBR-WEO <GO>` (IMF World Economic Outlook, April 2026): growth 0.8% in 2026 and 1.3% in 2027, inflation
2.4% in 2027, government debt 102.9% of GDP in 2030. The record mixes outturns (2015 to 2025: −10.0% growth in 2020, 9.1% inflation in
2022) with projections; each row says which.

**Its peers.** `ECG ECG-HIC <GO>`, computed from its 84 member economies when opened: GDP 75,910 bn USD, growth 1.88% (weighted by
GDP), median inflation 2.34%. The UK grows more slowly and its prices rise faster than the high-income median. `ECG ECG-WLD <GO>`: 213
economies, 117,327 bn USD, growth 2.80%.

**Debt, twice, one figure.** Debt appears on the economy record and on the fiscal record, and both carry the same year:
`ECON ECON-GBR` and `FISC FISC-GBR <GO>` both say 102.3% of GDP for 2025 (the United States: 123.9% on both). The economy record takes
the IMF series' latest *actual* year, never a projection; the path beyond 2025 is on the forecast, `FCST FCST-GBR-WEO <GO>`.

## 5. Questions to ask your data

| Question | Ask | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| What economy does this counterparty depend on? | `CPTY CP-ALDERSHOT <GO>` → its Economy link | the country of risk and its economy, one click away |
| How is the UK economy doing? | `ECON ECON-GBR <GO>` | GDP, growth, inflation, unemployment, debt, current account, output by sector |
| Where is it heading? | `FCST FCST-GBR-WEO <GO>` | the IMF's projections of growth, inflation and debt, by vintage |
| How does it compare with its peers? | `ECG ECG-HIC <GO>` | the income group's totals, GDP-weighted growth, median inflation, every member |
| What is the whole world doing? | `ECG ECG-WLD <GO>` | world GDP, population, growth and inflation from every economy |
| Who governs the country, and when is the next vote? | `JUR JUR-GBR <GO>` | the government, office holders, legislature and elections |
| How heavy is the state's debt? | `FISC FISC-GBR <GO>` | debt, deficit and revenue as shares of GDP |

## 6. Common confusions

**Nominal and real.** GDP in current US dollars rises with prices and exchange rates; real growth removes inflation and measures
output. A country can show rising dollar GDP while its economy shrinks.

**A group's growth is weighted, its inflation is a median.** Drishti weights a group's growth by each member's GDP, so large economies
count more; it takes the median of the members' inflation (as the World Bank does), so one country with runaway prices does not
dominate.

**Outturn and projection.** The forecast record mixes the past (outturn) and the IMF's projections. Each IMF edition (the vintage)
revises them; say which vintage you quote.

**Country of risk, country, jurisdiction.** A counterparty's economy link follows its country of risk, which may differ from where it
is registered. See [Entities and ownership](entities-and-ownership.md#6-common-confusions).

**Internal use only.** IMF World Economic Outlook data is for internal demonstrations; ask before showing it outside the bank. World
Bank and CIA Factbook data are free to reuse.

**Stored or computed?** Each economy's figures are fundamental, as published. Country groups are computed from their members when
opened since 2.9.2; the stored group pages were retired.

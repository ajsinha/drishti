## 1. The idea in plain words

A **company** issues shares; investors own them either directly or, far more often, through a **fund**. To see who owns a company,
how much of a portfolio really sits in one name, or what a fund costs, you need both sides and the identifiers that tie them together.

The company side has several names for the same thing. A **ticker** (`AAPL`) is how a share trades; one company can have several, one
per **share class** (Alphabet's `GOOGL` carries votes, `GOOG` none). The SEC knows every filer by its **CIK**, a ten-digit number that
never changes, and files it under a four-digit **SIC code** (3571, electronic computers) that rolls up into a **SIC division** (D,
Manufacturing). The world knows the legal entity by its **LEI** ([Entities and ownership](entities-and-ownership.md)). Everything the
company files, annual 10-K, quarterly 10-Q, 8-K events, insiders' Form 4, is published on **EDGAR**, and its accounts come tagged as
XBRL **financial facts**.

A fund pools investors' money and buys a portfolio. A **mutual fund** deals once a day with the fund itself, at its **NAV** (net asset
value: what it owns less what it owes, per share). An **ETF** trades on an exchange all day like a share; large dealers create and
redeem its shares in blocks, which keeps its price near the NAV. Either may have several share classes, one portfolio sold at
different fees: the **expense ratio** is that yearly fee, in percent of assets. An **index fund** copies an index; its **active share**
says how far it strays from the index's **weights**.

Every US fund reports every holding to the SEC each month on **Form N-PORT**. That makes a **look-through** possible: open the fund and
see each company it holds and how much, or open a company and see every fund that **holds** it. Two funds that hold the same companies
at similar weights **overlap**; owning both is less diversified than it looks.

Why a bank cares:

- **Exposure through funds.** A desk that holds an ETF holds its companies. Look-through counts NVIDIA held through QQQ as NVIDIA.
- **Identifiers.** Ticker, CIK, CUSIP, ISIN and LEI each answer a different system; joining them correctly is most of the work.
- **Ownership.** The 200 funds of the demo data alone hold about a fifth of Apple's, NVIDIA's and Microsoft's shares: who they are,
  and how concentrated, matters to an issuer, a lender and a trader.
- **Cost and tracking.** The classes of one S&P 500 fund cost from 0.01% to 0.14% a year, and two funds' holdings can differ by more
  than their names say.

## 3. How the data connects

```text
The companies (us-equities pack: SEC EDGAR and N-PORT, Wikidata, GLEIF, Nasdaq Trader; public)
us-company       USEQ <ticker>          CIK, SIC code and division, LEI, other tickers, shares outstanding, ten years of financials
   │  SIC_SECTOR ─► sic-sector  SIC <code>              computed: the code's companies by revenue (SIC ALL: every code)
   │  INDEX_IDS ─► index-membership  IDX <index>        SP500, NDX, DJIA (SPY, QQQ, DIA holdings); BANKS-US, BANKS-CA, BANKS-UK (Drishti's lists)
   │  FILING_IDS ─► filing  FILING <accession>          FACT_IDS ─► financial-fact  FACT <ticker>-<line>
   │  ECONOMY ─► economy  ECON ECON-<ISO3>              MARKET_WATCH ─► listed-company
   │  LEI (a field) ═ lei-entity  LEI <code>            (gleif pack: its US_COMPANY links back; LEIG its ownership group)
filing-feed      EDGAR <form>           the latest filings of one form: ALL, 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, 4, DEF-14A

The funds (funds pack: SEC Form N-PORT, Form N-CEN, prospectus risk/return summaries; public)
fund             FUND <ticker>          type, share classes, net assets, expense ratio, adviser, holdings, the fund against its index
   │  HOLDINGS.COMPANY ─► us-company  USEQ             HOLDINGS.FUND ─► fund  (a fund of funds)
   │  INDEX_ID, VS_INDEX.INDEX_ID ─► index-membership  IDX
   │  LEI, ADVISER_LEI, CUSTODIAN_LEI, HOLDINGS.LEI, HOLDINGS.CPTY_LEI, GLEIF_RELATIONSHIPS.LEI ─► lei-entity  LEI
fund-holder      HELD <ticker>          every fund holding one company: value, weight, share of its shares (USEQ's Held by funds tab)
   │  COMPANY ─► us-company   HOLDERS.FUND ─► fund
index-funds      TRACK <index>          the funds tracking SP500, NDX or DJIA (IDX's ETFs and funds tracking it)
   │  ROWS.FUND ─► fund
```

The links are the contract link columns of `config/packs/us-equities/contracts/`, `config/packs/funds/contracts/` and
`config/packs/gleif/contracts/`. The us-equities and funds packs are **off by default**: add `us-equities,gleif,funds` to
`DRISHTI_PACKS` ([Funds and ETFs guide](../guides/PACK_DEVELOPER_GUIDE.md#1-turning-the-pack-on)).

**How a holding finds its company.** A fund files each holding with an issuer name, a CUSIP, often an ISIN and an issuer LEI, rarely a
ticker. The funds pack matches it to a us-equities company by ticker, else (an equity) by a CUSIP another fund filed with the ticker, the
issuer's LEI, or its name. Both of Alphabet's classes (CUSIPs `02079K305` and `02079K107`) map to the one company `GOOGL`.

**Id conventions.** A company is its main ticker as the SEC writes it, a class after a hyphen (`AAPL`, `BRK-B`); its other tickers are
in `OTHER_TICKERS`. A CIK has ten digits (`0000320193`). A SIC code has four (`3674`); a division is a letter (`D`). A fact is
`<ticker>-<line>` (`NVDA-REVENUE`). A filing is its accession number (`0001104659-26-117587`). A fund is the ticker of its ETF class
where it has one, else its oldest class; any class's ticker opens it (`FUND VFIAX` opens VOO). An SEC series is `S` and nine digits
(`S000002839`), a share class `C` and nine digits. Indexes are `SP500`, `NDX`, `DJIA`.

## 4. A worked example on the demo data

The committed public snapshots: us-equities retrieved 2026-10-24 (**586 companies**, 194 SIC codes in 9 divisions, 531 with an LEI; the index members as SPY, QQQ and DIA reported holding them);
funds retrieved 2026-10-26 (the **200 largest US mutual funds and ETFs**, 24,593bn USD of net assets: 117 mutual funds, 50 ETFs, 31
mutual funds with an ETF share class, 2 unit investment trusts; N-PORT reports dated 2026-05-31 to 2026-07-31); GLEIF retrieved
2026-10-25 (9,937 legal entities). These records are not dated by business date: every date reads the same snapshot.

**One company, five identifiers.** `USEQ AAPL <GO>` is *Apple Inc.*: ticker AAPL, CIK `0000320193`, SIC 3571 (Electronic Computers),
division D (Manufacturing), LEI `HWUPKR0MPOU8FGXBT394`, member of the Dow, the Nasdaq-100 and the S&P 500, 14,776,353,000 shares
outstanding, revenue 416.2bn USD in fiscal 2025. Its LEI opens the GLEIF record and its ownership group (`LEIG HWUPKR0MPOU8FGXBT394`,
21 entities). Its industry: `SIC 3674 <GO>` has the 20 semiconductor companies, NVIDIA first by revenue (215.9bn), then Micron
(133.2bn) and Broadcom (63.9bn). By division the 586 companies are D Manufacturing 234, H Finance, Insurance and Real Estate 132, I
Services 87, E Transportation, Communications and Utilities 66, G Retail Trade 33, B Mining 13, F Wholesale 11, C Construction 9 and A
Agriculture 1.

**Share classes, twice.** `USEQ GOOGL <GO>` is *Alphabet Inc.*, CIK `0001652044`, SIC 7370 (division I, Services), with other tickers
GOOG, GOOGM and GOOGN. In `FUND VOO`'s holdings Alphabet appears twice: class A 3.25% and class C 2.59% of net assets, together 5.84%,
both linked to `GOOGL`. Funds have classes too: `FUND VOO <GO>` (Vanguard 500 Index Fund) is one portfolio sold four ways:

| Class | Ticker | Expense ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Investor Shares | VFINX | 0.14% |
| Admiral Shares | VFIAX | 0.04% |
| ETF Shares | VOO | 0.03% |
| Institutional Select Shares | VFFSX | 0.01% |

110 of the 200 funds have more than one class; the Growth Fund of America (`FUND AGTHX`) has 21, from 0.29% to 1.39% a year.

**Net assets and what a fund owes.** `FUND QQQ <GO>` (Invesco QQQ, an ETF): total assets 492.5bn, net assets 490.1bn, so it owes
2.4bn; 105 holdings, expense ratio 0.18%, the top 10 weigh 45.00%, NVIDIA 7.60% is the largest. `FUND PONAX` (PIMCO Income Fund) owns
503.6bn but its net assets are 230.7bn: it owes more than half of what it holds. Its largest holding is a to-be-announced mortgage
purchase (*UMBS, TBA*, 11.34% of net assets) it has yet to pay for, beside 1,730 derivative positions with 38 counterparties.

**Index funds and their fees.** `TRACK SP500 <GO>`: seven funds track the S&P 500, four of them ETFs, 4,821.3bn together. Their
expense ratios: FXAIX 0.01%, SPYM 0.02%, SWPPX 0.02%, VOO 0.03%, IVV 0.03%, VINIX 0.03%; SPY, a unit investment trust, files none.
Each shows an **active share** of 5.3% to 5.8% against the computed index weights, not 0 (the reason is under *What the data hasn't
told you*). An active fund for comparison: `FUND AGTHX` has an active share of 44.4% against the S&P 500. `TRACK DJIA`: DIA, 1.8%,
because the Dow weighs its members by share price and Drishti weighs them the same way.

**Concentration.** `FUND VGT <GO>` (Vanguard Information Technology): NVIDIA 16.82%, Apple 14.57%, Microsoft 9.46%; top 10 59.36%,
Herfindahl index 0.066. `FUND VTI` (Vanguard Total Stock Market, the largest fund at 2,297.8bn): 3,546 holdings, top 10 32.03%,
Herfindahl 0.015. The page keeps VTI's 600 largest holdings; the other 2,946 weigh 7.99%.

**Look-through from the company.** `HELD AAPL <GO>`: 85 funds (26 ETFs) hold 831.0bn of Apple, **19.02% of its shares outstanding**,
at an average weight of 5.43% and at most 14.57% (VGT). `HELD NVDA`: 87 funds, 1,043.7bn, 21.19% of its shares, at most 22.47% of a
fund (FSPTX). Microsoft is held by the most funds, 91.

**Overlap.** Drishti has no overlap command yet; the figure below is read from two funds' holdings: for each us-equities company both
hold, the smaller of its two weights, added up (computed from the committed `fund` snapshot, weights summed over a company's lines).

| Funds | Companies in common | Overlap | Read it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPY and VOO | 494 | 99.4% | the same index, the same month: one portfolio |
| VOO and IVV | 494 | 99.3% | the same |
| QQQ and QQQM | 92 | 91.2% | the same index, but reports a month apart (2026-06-30, 2026-05-31) |
| VOO and VTI | 448 | 87.2% | at least: VTI's page keeps 600 of its 3,546 holdings |
| QQQ and VOO | 85 | 54.3% | half of QQQ is already in an S&P 500 fund |
| VOO and AGTHX | 166 | 51.9% | an active growth fund, half of it the index |
| QQQ and VGT | 39 | 50.6% | different names, the same few technology companies |

None reaches 100% because a weight is of net assets, and every fund also holds cash, futures and companies outside the us-equities
pack.

**From fund to GLEIF.** `FUND VOO`'s GLEIF relationships: *managed by* The Vanguard Group, Inc. (`LEI 5493002789CX3L0CJP65 <GO>`, in
the snapshot) and *sub-fund of* its umbrella (`549300G6KNDK44WUN559`). The advisers' LEIs of 77 of the 193 funds that report one are in the GLEIF snapshot,
and 486 of the 531 companies' LEIs (the 2.9.3 additions are not yet in it); none of the 200 funds' own LEIs is (the snapshot was
chosen by ownership group, not by fund).

**What the data hasn't told you.** An index fund should show an active share near 0, yet the S&P 500 funds show 5.3% to 5.8%
and `FUND QQQ` 11.2% against the Nasdaq-100, though both hold almost only members (QQQ 99.2% of its assets). The members are right:
they are what the tracking funds report holding. The *weights* are the gap: Drishti computes an index weight as shares outstanding
times price, and 55 companies have no usable share count in the SEC data, Alphabet, Meta, Berkshire Hathaway and Palantir among them
(a company with several share classes may report one count per class). They are left out of the computed weights (each *vs index* tab
lists them), so the fund's weight in them shows as a difference. `HELD GOOGL` therefore shows 85 funds and 801.9bn but no share of
shares outstanding. The Dow, weighted by price, needs no share count: DIA's 1.8% is the honest figure. Read an index fund's active
share in Drishti as a check on the share counts as much as on the fund.

Until 2.9.3 the members came from Wikidata, whose lists were out of date: its Nasdaq-100 had 77 names without NVIDIA and Meta, its
S&P 500 459 names without Exxon Mobil or BlackRock, and its Dow 34. QQQ then showed an active share of 26.75%. The fix was to take the
members from the funds that must hold them.

## 6. Common confusions

**Ticker, share class, company.** A ticker names one class of a security; a company or fund can have several. Drishti keys a company
by one ticker (`GOOGL`, with GOOG in `OTHER_TICKERS`) and a fund by one class (`VOO`; `FUND VFIAX` opens it). Add up a company's
classes before you compare weights: Alphabet is 5.84% of VOO, not 3.25%.

**Mutual fund, ETF, unit investment trust.** A mutual fund deals with its investors at the day's NAV; an ETF's shares trade on an
exchange at a market price close to the NAV; a unit investment trust is a fixed trust, and SPY and DIA are ETFs built that way.
Vanguard's funds are mutual funds *with an ETF share class*: VOO is a class of the Vanguard 500 Index Fund, not a separate fund.

**NAV, net assets, total assets.** Net assets are what the fund owns less what it owes; NAV is that per share. Total assets leave the
debts in, so a leveraged or forward-buying fund (PONAX: 503.6bn total, 230.7bn net) looks twice its size. Weights are always in percent
of **net** assets, so a fund's weights can add up to more than 100% (PONAX's: 150.01%). The funds pack has no NAV per share or price history.

**An expense ratio of 0.03 is 0.03%, not 3%.** Weights, ratios and returns are in percent as filed.

**Index fund does not mean active share 0.** Index weights are proprietary; Drishti computes them from SEC data over the members the
tracking ETF holds: market capitalisation (share count times the price the filings imply), capped as the Nasdaq-100's methodology caps
it, or by share price for the Dow. A company with no usable share count, or several share classes, raises the figure without the fund
changing anything; the members are as of the tracking ETF's report date, so a change to the index shows up to a quarter late.

**Reports a month apart.** Funds file for their own fiscal quarter, so `REPORT_DATE` differs (90 funds at 2026-06-30, 58 at 2026-07-31,
52 at 2026-05-31). Comparing two funds compares two dates: QQQ and QQQM, one index, overlap 85.5%.

**The fund's LEI, the registrant's, the adviser's.** A fund (an SEC series) has its own LEI; so has the trust it belongs to (the
registrant) and the firm that manages it (the adviser). GLEIF links them as *sub-fund of* and *managed by*; an ownership group (`LEIG`)
follows accounting consolidation and does not include the funds a manager runs.

**SIC is not GICS.** The SEC's SIC codes date from 1937 and put Apple in Manufacturing; the GICS sectors of index providers would put
it in Information Technology. GICS is proprietary; Drishti shows SIC.

**US company or issuer?** A us-equities company (`USEQ`) is a real SEC filer from public data. An issuer (`ISS`,
[Entities and ownership](entities-and-ownership.md)) is the bank's own record of who stands behind a bond or CDS it trades, in the core
pack. They join on the LEI where a real issuer is in both.

**Stored or computed?** Companies, filings, facts and funds are fundamental, as the SEC published them. `SIC` is computed from the
companies when opened. The fund pages' concentration, index weights and active share, and the `HELD` and `TRACK` pages, are worked out
from the filings when the pack's snapshot is built (`tools/packgen/funds`), not when opened; a refresh rebuilds them.

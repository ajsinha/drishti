What is {{ PRODUCT }}

{{ PRODUCT }} ({{ PRODUCT_NATIVE or 'दृष्टि' }}, Sanskrit for sight) lets a person see a trade, a counterparty, a fund or an economy the same way: one live page, linked to what it relates to, showing only what that person may see. A short screen grammar called Rachana describes a view, a Sutra is a screen written in it, and inference completes whatever a Sutra leaves out. Above the trades, {{ PRODUCT }} computes risk when it is asked (value at risk, expected shortfall and counterparty exposure) for any set of trades you can describe, and never stores a computed figure as a summary.

The problem it solves

Every system has its own screens, built one by one, with their own access rules. Risk is published overnight for the hierarchy it was set up with, so a new question waits for a batch. Figures rarely say what they mean, where they came from or whether they were delivered or computed. {{ PRODUCT }} replaces the per-product screen with a grammar, computes risk for the scope you ask about from the trade-level inputs your engines already produce, and gives every command a data contract, so you can see what your data has not told you yet.

Key features

The command line

Type a mnemonic and a key (TRD MX-20000001, CPTY CP-ALDERSHOT) with suggestions as you type, or plain words; any entity over time (TRD 1000015 30). FUNC lists every command you can use; WHAT <term> explains a term.

VaR for any scope

Historical VaR and expected shortfall for the bank, a desk, a portfolio, a counterparty or any mix of portfolios, trades and rules: diversification applied, drill down to a trade, captures, backtesting, a what-if and a selection tray to build and save your own mix.

Counterparty exposure

EE, PFE, EPE and EEPE from simulated paths, netted per netting set with each CSA's collateral path by path; from the bank to one trade, its netting benefit and an exact pre-deal check.

A data contract for every command

Every kind of record has a checked contract (every column, unit and meaning; delivered or computed), and every pack's guide says where each kind's data comes from.

Knowledge

Pages that explain each domain (market risk, counterparty credit risk and more) in plain words, with a glossary, how the records connect and worked examples on your own data.

Sutras and the designer

A screen is a short Rachana (Sutra) file built from a library of fragments: events, child panels, forms, refresh and layout, designed in four modes and shipped without code.

Query by form

Filter, sort and pick from a form with the same power as the command line, answering in a fraction of a second over tens of millions of trades.

Write-back

Forms save through an edit log with four-eyes approval and recompute the views that depend on the change. Off by default.

Live updates

Views tick as the data changes; only what changed is sent; computed panels recompute on loads and on your controls.

Any connector as a file

Delta Lake, Iceberg, PostgreSQL, Aerospike, Cassandra and ScyllaDB, MongoDB, Kafka and more, added from a file: TLS, proxy and secrets are settings and no restart is needed.

Domain packs

Core reference data, market data, trading (production-shaped trades from ENDUR, CALYPSO and MUREX), market and counterparty risk, the world economy, news and markets, US equities, GLEIF ownership, and US funds and ETFs.

External data, named and offline

SEC filings and fund holdings, GLEIF, public macro and market data: each source with its licence and a refresh script for Linux and Windows, and a snapshot that runs with no internet.

Functions and switches

One directory of every command for everyone; administrators switch a command off everywhere (page, API, CLI, search, exports, BI and AI) from the same page.

Masks and roles everywhere

One mask decides what each person sees, in views, search, exports, reports, notebooks, risk answers and the AI layer.

Rupaka BI

Models and datasets rooted in the packs, including the official VaR and exposure results; a Python-like expression language and SQL; reports and boards in BI Studio; governance and review.

Python, where it belongs

Python measures, visuals and Transforms, sandboxed; risk engines on warm compute pools; your own Python in the browser on a masked extract.

Reports, alerts and the inbox

Scheduled reports and statements, page-exact in PDF and Excel, one per recipient with their own masks; rules that raise alerts; share and discuss a finding.

{{ PRODUCT }}*net

Several servers act as one: any of them answers any request with the caller's masks, and the console moves to another server when one stops, with no new sign-in.

{{ PRODUCT }} Elements and AI

Embed a view, report or panel in another page with one tag. The AI layer is off by default and, when on, sees only what the person may see.

Ten themes

Terminal, Parchment, Wall Street, Blue, Green, Crimson and Crimson dark, and the Terminal look in green, crimson or blue; every text colour checked at 4.5:1.

Competitive advantage

Terminals show market data, platform screens show one vendor's trades, risk systems publish overnight for a fixed hierarchy, low-code tools need a screen built per table, and BI tools stop at the chart. {{ PRODUCT }} is one grammar for all of them, with risk computed for the question you ask and every figure explained: live, linked, masked, self-hosted and keyboard-first.

Technology stack

  • Server: one Spring Boot 3.5 application on Java 21 or newer with virtual threads; a Spring-free plugin SPI for sources; DuckDB for BI queries.
  • Console: FastAPI and server-rendered pages; Bootstrap, Bootstrap Icons, ECharts, Vega-Lite, Graphic Walker, CodeMirror and Pyodide, all vendored; a sandboxed Python runner for BI.
  • Cluster: {{ PRODUCT }}*net, membership by gossip over HTTP with calls signed by a cluster key, off by default.
  • Security: a strict content-security policy with no inline script or style, so it runs air-gapped; roles and masks enforced on the server.

Use cases

  • Trading desks. Trades, positions and P&L as linked live views, with the netting set one key away and the VaR of any mix of portfolios in seconds.
  • Market risk. VaR and ES for the scope in question, its contributions and its backtest, before and after a trade.
  • Credit risk. A counterparty's exposure across its netting sets, what collateral does to it, and the effect of a new trade before it is booked.
  • Research. Companies, filings, funds and their holdings, ownership groups and the world economy, with sources and licences named.
  • Learning the business. Knowledge pages and WHAT explain each term on the bank's own data.
  • Anywhere data lives. Point a connector at a system and get views the same day, with no screen coded.
